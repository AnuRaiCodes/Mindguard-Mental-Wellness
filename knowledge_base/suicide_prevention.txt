SUICIDE PREVENTION AWARENESS — SAFE MESSAGING GUIDELINES
=========================================================

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
This document is for awareness and prevention education only. The AI assistant cannot predict suicide, diagnose mental illness, or replace professional mental health care. If you or someone you know is in immediate danger, call emergency services (112 in India) immediately.

UNDERSTANDING SUICIDE PREVENTION
Suicide is a serious public health concern. In India, approximately 164,000 deaths by suicide occur each year (NCRB data). However, suicidal crises are often temporary and treatable. With the right support, people can and do recover.

RISK FACTORS (Warning Signs)
Awareness of warning signs helps friends, family, and communities provide timely support:
• Talking about wanting to die or to kill oneself
• Looking for ways to kill oneself
• Talking about being a burden to others
• Expressing feelings of hopelessness or having no reason to live
• Talking about being trapped or in unbearable pain
• Increased use of alcohol or drugs
• Withdrawing from friends, family, and activities
• Changing eating and sleeping habits
• Showing rage, extreme mood swings
• Giving away prized possessions
• Saying goodbye as if it were final

IMPORTANT: The presence of these signs does not mean a person WILL attempt suicide, but they are signals to take seriously and respond with compassion.

PROTECTIVE FACTORS
Factors that reduce suicide risk:
• Strong social connections and support networks
• Access to mental health care
• Reasons for living (family, children, pets, faith, goals)
• Sense of purpose and belonging
• Problem-solving skills and coping strategies
• Restricted access to lethal means
• Cultural and religious beliefs against suicide

HOW TO SUPPORT SOMEONE IN DISTRESS
If you are concerned about someone:
1. Ask directly but gently: "Are you thinking about suicide?" — Research shows asking does NOT plant the idea
2. Listen without judgment — don't try to argue or minimize their pain
3. Stay with them — don't leave a person in acute crisis alone
4. Remove access to means if possible (medications, sharp objects)
5. Help connect them to professional support
6. Follow up — check in regularly after the initial crisis

WHAT NOT TO SAY
• "You have so much to live for" (invalidates their pain)
• "Think of how this will affect your family" (increases guilt)
• "You're being selfish" (shameful and counterproductive)
• "You don't really mean that" (dismissive)
• "Things could be worse" (minimizing)

WHAT TO SAY INSTEAD
• "I hear you. I'm here for you."
• "I care about you. Your pain is real."
• "You don't have to face this alone."
• "Would you be willing to talk to someone who can help?"
• "I want to help you stay safe."

SUICIDE PREVENTION IN INDIA — SPECIFIC CONSIDERATIONS
• Stigma: Many families in India are reluctant to discuss suicidal feelings openly. Creating a safe, non-judgmental space is critical.
• Academic pressure: Student suicides are a significant concern — NEET, board exams, competitive entrance pressure
• Farmer distress: Rural communities face unique economic and social stressors
• Gender: Men are less likely to seek help; women face relationship and domestic pressures
• Technology: Social media can both increase and reduce risk — online communities can provide support

SAFE MESSAGING GUIDELINES (for media, educators, helpers)
• DO describe suicide as a serious public health issue
• DO report available crisis resources
• DO encourage help-seeking and emphasize treatment works
• DO NOT describe the method used in detail
• DO NOT sensationalize or romanticize suicide
• DO NOT present suicide as a solution to problems
• DO NOT use language like "successful suicide" (use "died by suicide")
• DO NOT speculate on the reason

AFTER A SUICIDE LOSS (Postvention)
Losing someone to suicide is devastating. Grief may include shock, guilt, anger, and confusion.
• Seek support from grief counselors experienced in suicide loss
• Connect with survivor support groups
• Allow yourself to grieve without self-blame
• Resources: Alliance of Hope for Suicide Loss Survivors
